Evidence-Based Prostate Management

Understanding Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia (BPH)

The prostate is a walnut-sized gland that surrounds the urethra just below the bladder in men. As men age, hormonal shifts typically cause the prostate to enlarge — a non-cancerous condition termed Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia (BPH).

When the enlarged tissue compresses the urethra, it impedes normal urine flow, leading to troublesome lower urinary tract symptoms (LUTS) that can disturb sleep, work, and daily activities.

Common Symptoms of an Enlarged Prostate

  • Nocturia: Waking up multiple times each night to urinate
  • Weak Urinary Stream: Reduced force or interrupted urine flow
  • Urgency & Hesitancy: Sudden urge to urinate, or difficulty starting the stream
  • Incomplete Emptying: Feeling that the bladder has not fully emptied
  • Urinary Retention: Inability to pass urine, requiring medical intervention

Diagnostic Workup for Prostate Health

An accurate clinical evaluation includes digital rectal examination (DRE), Serum PSA blood test (to screen for prostatic conditions), Uroflowmetry (measuring urine flow rate), and Ultrasound KUB with Post-Void Residual (PVR) measurement.

Laser Prostate Surgery & Conventional Options

For very large prostates or severe obstruction, surgical enucleation or resection may be advised:

  • Holmium Laser Enucleation of the Prostate (HoLEP): High-power laser enucleates the entire obstructing adenoma with minimal bleeding, ideal for larger prostates (>80-100cc).
  • Bipolar TURP: The gold-standard endoscopic procedure using bipolar saline energy to resect obstructing prostate tissue with high safety.
  • Medication & Conservative Management: Alpha-blockers (tamsulosin, silodosin) and 5-alpha reductase inhibitors (finasteride, dutasteride) for early-stage symptom relief.
