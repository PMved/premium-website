Advanced Laser Kidney Stone Treatment & RIRS

Modern incision-free laser lithotripsy, flexible endourology, and personalized dietary prevention protocols for kidney and ureteric stones in Indore.

Minimally Invasive Endourology

Understanding Kidney Stones (Renal Calculi)

Kidney stones are hard mineral deposits that form inside the renal calyces when urine becomes concentrated with crystal-forming substances such as calcium, oxalate, and uric acid.

While small stones may pass spontaneously, larger stones can lodge in the ureter or renal pelvis, blocking urine flow and causing intense loin-to-groin pain (renal colic), blood in the urine (hematuria), nausea, and potential kidney infection.

Accurate Diagnostic Evaluation

Prompt diagnosis prevents kidney parenchymal injury. Dr. Chipde utilizes high-resolution Non-Contrast CT KUB (Kidney, Ureter, Bladder), the international gold standard to precisely map stone size, density (Hounsfield units), and anatomical location, accompanied by renal function tests and urine cultures.

Modern Laser & Endoscopic Stone Treatments

1. RIRS (Retrograde Intrarenal Surgery)

A state-of-the-art procedure performed without any skin cuts. A flexible digital ureteroscope is guided naturally through the urethra and bladder into the innermost chambers of the kidney. A high-power Holmium or Thulium laser fiber is then used to pulverize the stone into fine dust (laser dusting), which washes out naturally.

  • No surgical cuts or external scars
  • Daycare or overnight hospital stay
  • Quick recovery with return to work in 24–48 hours
  • Ideal for renal stones up to 20mm and difficult lower pole stones

2. Mini-PCNL (Keyhole Percutaneous Nephrolithotomy)

For larger stones (>20mm), staghorn calculi, or multiple stones. A miniature puncture (less than 5mm) is made through the flank directly into the kidney under fluoroscopic guidance to fragment and evacuate the stone fragments directly.

3. Ureteroscopy (URS) for Ureteric Stones

For stones lodged in the middle or lower ureter, rigid or semi-rigid ureteroscopy combined with laser energy rapidly clears the obstruction, providing immediate pain relief.

4. Medical Expulsive Therapy (MET)

For small, uncomplicated stones under 5mm located near the bladder, medications (alpha-blockers) and adequate hydration may be prescribed under regular ultrasound monitoring.

Prevention & Dietary Guidance

Stopping Stone Recurrence

Without lifestyle changes, over 50% of stone formers develop another stone within 5 years. Dr. Chipde provides structured metabolic counseling:

  • Target Hydration: Drink sufficient fluids to achieve at least 2.5 liters of clear urine daily.
  • Sodium Moderation: High dietary salt drives calcium into urine; restrict processed foods.
  • Balanced Calcium: Do not restrict dietary calcium unnecessarily; normal dietary calcium binds oxalate in the gut.
  • Citrate Intake: Lemon water and citrus juices help raise urinary citrate, a natural inhibitor of stone crystals.

Kidney Stone FAQs

A temporary Double-J (DJ) stent is commonly placed for 7 to 14 days after RIRS to ensure the ureter remains open and prevent post-procedure colic while residual stone dust passes. The stent is quickly removed in OPD under local anesthesia.
Severe unrelenting pain with high fever/chills, inability to pass urine, persistent vomiting, or a stone in a patient with a solitary kidney constitutes a medical emergency requiring urgent decompression.
